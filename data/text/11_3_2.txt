Jean-Marc-Gaspard Itard, a young doctor, was convinced, however, that the boy would be helped, since he believed that his low intelligence was caused by his solitary life in the woods from about the age of seven and by his ignorance of language. Itard took the boy and worked with him for five years. Victor, as Itard called him, did not learn to speak French, but he was able to understand a large number of written words and phrases. He would obey simple written commands and could use word cards to show his desires. He also acquired the manners and appearance of a civilized young men. Victor had obviously been helped by education, but Itard finally concluded that Victor could not be further improved because his intelligence was subnormal.
